# Sell crypto, show every dollar, claim nothing

An original premium US crypto-to-USD exchange can stand out most by doing what Coinbase, Kraken and Cash App do not. It should merge the industry's two-step "sell, then withdraw" journey into **one quote, one confirmation and one status tracker**. That quote should state the market rate, the customer's rate, the spread in dollars and percent, every fee, and the exact USD received, all on the main card and none of it in a tooltip. The visual language should be calm: warm white, navy ink, emerald used sparingly, and tabular figures. That deliberately rejects the dark-neon, glassmorphism and 3D-coin look that crypto galleries are full of and that reads as "token launch," not "regulated money movement." Trust has to come from specifics that can be checked: the legal entity named, a licenses page, payout times given as hedged ranges, and risk and scam warnings. Adjectives, badges, invented testimonials and anything that implies FDIC coverage do not build trust, and US regulators have penalized all of them. Because the site runs on mock data and holds no licenses, the most important trust feature is honesty about that. It needs a persistent "demonstration only" banner, placeholders such as "NMLS ID: none," and courier cash shown as "concept only, not offered." Courier cash delivery is the riskiest feature under the Bank Secrecy Act, and the research found no US precedent of a licensed exchange offering it. Large tickets should go to a request-for-quote path that defaults to bank wire. **This report is not legal advice.** Every regulatory point needs review by qualified US counsel before any real-money launch.

> **What this rests on.** The network policy in the research environment blocked every live reference site: coinbase.com, kraken.com, cash.app, dribbble.com, behance.net, and the primary regulatory sites (fincen.gov, ofac.treasury.gov, dfpi.ca.gov, dfs.ny.gov, ftc.gov, ecfr.gov). Direct fetches failed with DNS errors or proxy 403s, and the block was not worked around. **Every finding below comes from web-search result snippets and the search engine's summaries of the cited pages. No page was read in full, and no screen, layout or gallery shot was seen.** Quoted wording is snippet text and may be cut off. Visual specifics such as hex values, typefaces and sizes for Coinbase, Kraken, Cash App, Wise and Mercury come mostly from unofficial, community-built design-token write-ups that disagree with each other. Treat every figure as "verify against the live page or the primary text."

## Leaders split cash-out in two; an off-ramp should make it one

Both market leaders now sell breadth rather than a single job. Kraken's 2026 homepage, as indexed, leads with "Own the power of your money" and "Crypto and stocks made easy," and its app is listed as "Kraken: Buy Crypto & Stocks" ([Kraken homepage](https://www.kraken.com/); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.kraken.invest.app&hl=en_US)). Coinbase's June 2026 messaging centers on "Take Control of Your Money," and that month it launched AI agent accounts that trade and spend for users ([Coinbase Blog](https://www.coinbase.com/blog); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/06/11/coinbase-launches-ai-agent-accounts-that-can-trade-and-spend-on-your-behalf)). Coinbase's hero headline, CTA labels, nav and footer structure could not be verified. For Kraken, only three nav items were confirmed. A focused off-ramp therefore has a clear opening: state the one job in plain words, along the lines of "Sell crypto. Get dollars."

The bigger opportunity is in the structure of the journey. Both platforms treat cashing out as **two separate transactions**: sell into a USD balance, then withdraw to a bank, with its own preview and confirmation at each step. Coinbase's own help article says the sale and the bank transfer appear separately ([Coinbase Help: Sell or cash out](https://help.coinbase.com/en/wallet/managing-account/sell-cash-out)). Kraken's app flow (+ → Sell → amount → Continue → Confirm) ends with "cash added to your balance," and withdrawal is a separate step ([Kraken Support](https://support.kraken.com/articles/360058727972-buying-selling-and-converting-cryptocurrency-with-the-kraken-app)). Cash App's flow is shorter: Money tab → Bitcoin → Sell → preset or custom amount → PIN → Confirm ([Cash App Help](https://cash.app/help/us/en-us/3102-selling-bitcoin)). An original product should go one step further. Its step indicator could be **Amount → Payout account → Review → Send crypto**, followed by a single tracker that ends with "USD arrives in your bank." Post-transaction receipts and status screens for all three reference brands were not documented in any accessible snippet, so the leaders' treatment there is unknown, not shown to be weak.

The information architecture also needs an early fork between retail and large-ticket users. Kraken and Coinbase both keep separate surfaces for pro and institutional users (Kraken Pro, /institutions OTC, the Krak money app; Coinbase /institutional and /prime) ([Kraken Pro](https://pro.kraken.com/); [Krak](https://krak.app/); [Coinbase Institutional](https://www.coinbase.com/institutional)). Kraken publishes a concrete **$50,000 OTC minimum** (lowered from $100K in March 2025, though some Kraken pages still show $100K). Its contact form starts from the trade itself, asking for base currency, quote currency and amount ([Kraken Blog](https://blog.kraken.com/news/otc-50k-minimum); [Kraken OTC form](https://www.kraken.com/institutions/otc/contactform); [Kraken USDC OTC](https://www.kraken.com/features/otc-exchange/usdc)). Coinbase Prime publishes no minimum. Third-party figures conflict and are unverified ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/coinbase-prime/pricing)). Starting the form from the trade is the stronger pattern for large retail tickets. The original site's large-transaction entry point can ask "what are you selling, and how much?" first, then the qualifying details. The threshold it shows should be labeled illustrative, not regulatory.

Search intent offers a final IA lesson. Kraken runs page families organized by intent, including `/learn/sell-{asset}-ach`, and both leaders run per-asset price pages ([Kraken "Sell … ACH"](https://www.kraken.com/learn/sell-alchemy-pay-ach); [Coinbase price page](https://www.coinbase.com/price/metahero)). Landing pages per asset and per payout rail fit an off-ramp well. One caution: for a site that runs on mock data, each such page needs to say clearly that its prices and rails are illustrative.

## Put the spread on the quote card, not in a tooltip

Hidden spread is the main transparency gap among the leaders. Coinbase shows fees "on the trade preview screen before submitting," but to see the spread users must "tap the tooltip next to your asset's price." Coinbase also reserves the right to "retain any excess spread" and publishes no fixed spread figure ([Coinbase fee disclosures](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/trading-and-funding/pricing-and-fees/fees)). NerdWallet says Coinbase's costs "can be difficult to track" ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/reviews/coinbase)). Kraken's confirmation page shows a "full fee breakdown," and its app-store copy promises "an exact price quote." Third-party estimates still put the simple app's all-in cost at about **2% (1% fee plus roughly 1% spread)** ([Kraken app FAQ](https://support.kraken.com/articles/360049744652-kraken-app-frequently-asked-questions); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1481947260); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/investing/review/kraken-review-130000202.html)). Cash App does show both its tiered service fee and its spread markdown on the confirmation screen ([Cash App Help 3103](https://cash.app/help/3103)). Yet its own pages disagree on the spread range, "0% and 1%" on one page and "0% and 0.75%" on another ([Cash App Help 3103](https://cash.app/help/3103); [cash.app/bitcoin/fees](https://cash.app/bitcoin/fees)). That is a cautionary example: **fee figures should come from one canonical source and be rendered everywhere from it**, so disclosures cannot drift apart.

There is also a regulatory reason to show the spread. California's first enforcement action under the Digital Financial Assets Law (DFAL), against Coinme in June 2025, cited receipts that left out required disclosures, including the spread between market price and quoted price ([DFPI](https://dfpi.ca.gov/press_release/dfpi-fines-coinme-300000-related-to-crypto-kiosk-violations-secures-51700-in-restitution-for-victims/); [Regulatory Oversight](https://www.regulatoryoversight.com/2025/07/californias-dfpi-brings-first-enforcement-action-under-californias-digital-financial-assets-law/)). That action involved kiosks, but the principle carries over. Wise is the model from outside crypto: it quotes the mid-market rate and charges a visible, separate fee rather than burying cost in the rate ([Wise compare](https://wise.com/compare/)). The quote card the notes converge on looks like this:

| Row | Example (mock) | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|
| Market reference rate | 1 USDC = $1.0000 | Lets the user see the spread instead of inferring it |
| Your rate | 1 USDC = $0.9980 | The rate actually applied |
| Spread | −$20.00 (0.20%) | Shown in both $ and %, never only in a tooltip |
| Network fee (est.) | ~$0.40 | Labeled as an estimate |
| Service fee | −$50.00 | Its own line |
| Payout fee | Free / $X by rail | Depends on the rail chosen |
| **You receive** | **$9,929.60** | The hero figure, separated by a hairline rule |
| Quote valid for | 0:28 · Refresh | Expiry with a one-tap requote |

Quote expiry needs a visible treatment. Coinbase's price API states that "the price is only correct for seconds." Coinbase Prime's RFQ holds a UI price for **3 seconds**, allows up to 20 refreshes, and expires the quote if it is not accepted ([Coinbase Developer Docs](https://docs.cdp.coinbase.com/coinbase-app/track-apis/prices); [Coinbase Help: RFQ](https://help.coinbase.com/en/prime/trading-and-funding/rfq)). Coinbase's equities flow already puts a Refresh control on the confirmation screen ([Coinbase Help: NBBO](https://help.coinbase.com/en/trading-and-funding/pricing-and-fees/nbbo-pricing-transparency)). No consumer countdown was verified for Coinbase, Kraken or Cash App. The "0:28 countdown plus Refresh" pattern is therefore a design proposal, not a copy of what the leaders do. When a quote expires, the inline message should read "Rate updated — review new amount" and require the user to confirm again. The quote change should be announced politely through `aria-live`.

Payout speed is now a priced product, and it should be shown as a choice. Kraken's US rails are **free ACH in 0–2 business days, Fedwire at $4 (with a $20 minimum), and Instant USD withdrawals since March 2026 at 1.5% capped at $50**, available 24/7 ([Kraken Support](https://support.kraken.com/articles/360000423043-cash-withdrawal-options-fees-minimums-and-processing-times-); [Kraken Blog](https://blog.kraken.com/news/instant-usd-withdrawals)). Cash App offers free standard transfers "within 1-3 business days" or instant transfers at "0.5% - 1.75%" with a $0.25 minimum. A third-party source claims different figures and a $75 cap, which could not be confirmed ([Cash App Help 3073](https://cash.app/help/3073-cash-out-speed-options); [eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/17345344-cash-app-fees-2026-instant-deposit-atm-and-business-charges)). Sources disagree on Coinbase's ACH timing (1–3 vs 3–5 business days), and its instant-cashout fee was not verified ([Coin Bureau](https://coinbureau.com/guides/how-to-withdraw-money-on-coinbase)). The pattern to take is a set of radio cards, each showing the fee in both $ and %, an arrival time hedged as a range ("typically," "usually within," "est."), and a footnote on what can cause delays. Cash App's bitcoin-withdrawal copy shows this discipline: "10 minutes or less," and network fees that "may differ from the estimate" ([Cash App Help 3106](https://cash.app/help/3106-bitcoin-withdrawals)). Kraken's $50 cap also shows that large-ticket users value capped fees. A public fee calculator (amount and rail in, spread, fees and USD received out) is something neither leader appears to publish. The notes could not see either leader's fee-page layout, so that absence is inferred, not confirmed.

## Calm light design beats the neon crypto template

The galleries are crowded with the look this product should avoid. Search summaries of Dribbble profiles, roundups and stock libraries show the same vocabulary: dark canvases, neon and purple-teal gradients, glassmorphism and 3D glass coins. Examples include "Dark Theme Crypto Trading Bot UI — Neon, Liquid Glass & Clay" and stock libraries holding tens of thousands of "crypto neon" assets ([Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/Creative_Web_Guru); [Shutterstock](https://www.shutterstock.com/search/crypto-neon); [A1 Gallery](https://www.a1.gallery/category/crypto)). Gradient vendors sell teal-to-violet blends made for "crypto exchange dark-mode dashboards" ([Pixeliro](https://pixeliro.com/gradients/cool--67eeec-9d4aeb--radial-315)). One critic puts it as "blur isn't depth" ([Medium](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/glassmorphism-the-most-beautiful-trap-in-modern-ui-design-a472818a7c0a)). One caveat applies: the Behance and Dribbble grids were never seen, so "dominance" rests on commentary and tags, not on a count. The patterns worth keeping from the galleries are few. They are single-purpose layouts focused on a decision, subtle confirmation micro-interactions, and explanations in plain language, which A1 Gallery names as what separates the stronger sites.

The leaders' discipline is more useful than their specific choices. Coinbase reportedly pairs a single blue accent, **#0052FF**, with white and near-black surfaces and custom faces from its 2022 Moniker rebrand. Kraken reportedly owns a purple around **#7132F5**. Cash App's late-2025 redesign added the proprietary Cash Sans, more than 650 icons, and a brighter green ([shadcn.io Coinbase](https://www.shadcn.io/design/coinbase); [Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/monikersf-coinbase-rebrand/); [shadcn.io Kraken](https://www.shadcn.io/design/kraken); [Abduzeedo](https://www.abduzeedo.com/cash-apps-new-design-language-and-ai-assistant-moneybot)). Every one of these values comes from unofficial reverse-engineering, and the sources conflict. Cash App's green appears as both #00e013 and #00d54b, and one source says Coinbase uses a system font stack. The lesson to keep is **one owned accent on a restrained canvas, one idea per section** ([landing.love](https://www.landing.love/sites/cash/)). An original brand should avoid all three hues and all proprietary faces. Wise Sans, Arcadia and the Coinbase faces cannot be licensed, and imitating them would undercut originality.

The notes propose a palette of warm white, navy and emerald. The hex values are original, and their WCAG contrast ratios were computed locally but not tested in a browser. The canvas is **#FAF8F4**, ink is navy **#0F1E36** (15.7:1 on canvas), and the accent is emerald-700 **#0B6B4C** (6.2:1). Neutrals take roughly 70–80% of each screen, navy 15–25%, and emerald no more than 5–10%. These ratios are derived from the 60-30-10 heuristic, not measured from any real site ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/60-30-10-rule/)). The most important move is a **navy-filled primary CTA rather than an emerald one**. That keeps "brand green" from being read as "success green," following the principle of separating brand tokens from functional tokens ([Groto](https://www.letsgroto.com/blog/60-30-10-rule)). Pending and processing states use an info blue (#2B4C8C), never amber or red. Errors use a brick red (#B42318). Status is never conveyed by hue alone, because emerald and red are easily confused by users with color-vision deficiency. The green-up / red-down trading convention should be avoided entirely. A light theme is itself a differentiator in this category, though the claim that "light reads as trust" rests on practitioner opinion and one audit whose method could not be checked, not on any controlled study ([LazyLanding Labs](https://lazylanding-labs.onrender.com/research/what-theme-do-finance-apps-launch-with.md)).

Typography carries the money. Every amount should use **tabular, lining figures** (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums`), which browsers have broadly supported since 2020. Browsers fall back silently when a font lacks `tnum`, so the chosen font must be checked ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/CSS/font-variant-numeric); [fontalternatives.com](https://fontalternatives.com/blog/best-fonts-legible-numerals-ui/)). The proposed setup is one open grotesque with `tnum` (Inter or Geist) for UI and figures, with an optional restrained open-licensed serif for marketing headlines only, never for figures. This echoes Mercury's grotesque-plus-serif split without copying it ([Blake Crosley on Mercury](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/mercury)). The "You receive" figure should be set at 40–48px desktop, weight 500–600. The currency code should be about half that size in a secondary color, numbers should be right-aligned in breakdown rows, and amounts should never be truncated. Monospace is reserved for addresses, hashes and reference IDs. One widely repeated claim, a "2025 Type Network study" showing that tabular figures raise trust, **could not be located and should not be cited** ([pravinkumar.co](https://www.pravinkumar.co/blog/tabular-numbers-webflow-pricing-tables-b2b-2026)).

The best component patterns come from production products and design-system documentation, not from gallery shots. The **network should be picked together with the token, before any address appears**. Kraken lists 11 networks for USDT. Coinbase preselects a default network and makes users acknowledge the risk before they switch. Bitso shows the address and QR code only after the network is chosen ([Kraken stablecoins](https://support.kraken.com/articles/stablecoins-supported-on-kraken); [Coinbase Help: multiple networks](https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/trading-and-funding/sending-or-receiving-cryptocurrency/assets-on-multiple-networks); [Bitso](https://support.bitso.com/hc/en-us/articles/4415146069780-Deposit-crypto-to-your-Bitso-account)). EVM chains share 0x address formats, so an address can be valid on the wrong chain with no error shown ([Copperx](https://copperx.io/learn/usdc-recovery)). The deposit card should therefore name the network in words ("Send only USDC on Ethereum") above an address chunked into groups of four characters. It should show the minimum, any memo or tag with its own copy button, and a calm note that a wrong-network deposit may be lost ([CryptoProcessing](https://docs.cryptoprocessing.com/deposits/design-payment-form)). Confirmation counts for the reference platforms conflict (Kraken's Ethereum USDT is listed as 20 or 30) and are unresolved ([eco.com](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/15247703-usdt-confirmation-time-by-network-2026)), so any ETA shown should be an estimate. The status timeline should be an ordered list: waiting for deposit, detected (0/N), confirmed, converting, payout sent, arrived (est.). A summary pill sits above it, and **"processing" must never look like "failed,"** because a pending payment can still complete ([Spendfigo](https://docs.spendfigo.com/documentation/transaction-lifecycle); [mctaba](https://mctaba.com/learn/paystack/pending-transactions-handling-the-paystack-grey-zone)). Steppers should use `aria-current="step"`, text labels alongside icons, 3–5 steps, and 4.5:1 contrast ([Telerik](https://www.telerik.com/kendo-react-ui/components/layout/stepper/accessibility/wai-aria-support); [Red Hat](https://ux.redhat.com/elements/progress-stepper/)). Sources disagree on the right container role for a stepper. Inline errors should be short and fix-oriented ("Minimum sell is $10.00"), linked to their field with `aria-describedby` ([Shopify Polaris](https://polaris.shopify.com/components/selection-and-input/inline-error); [Reform](https://www.reform.app/blog/accessible-form-validation-best-practices)). No primary design documentation from Robinhood, Wealthfront, Mercury or Stripe was retrieved, so these conventions come from general design-system guidance, not from those specific brands.

## Name the entity, and say plainly what the mock site lacks

All three reference brands build trust disclosure in two tiers. A short line repeats everywhere, and a detailed licenses page backs it up. Coinbase's Licenses & Disclosures page lists NMLS ID 1163082, an entry for each state and complaint contacts, and states that balances are not covered by FDIC, NCUA or SIPC ([Coinbase Licenses](https://www.coinbase.com/legal/licenses)). Kraken names its US money transmitter, **Payward Interactive, Inc.**, says it holds licenses in 46 jurisdictions, and uses a short line: "not a member of FINRA/SIPC and not FDIC insured" ([Kraken Disclosures](https://www.kraken.com/legal/disclosures); [Kraken Social Media Disclosure](https://www.kraken.com/social-media-disclosure)). Cash App repeats "a financial services platform, not a bank" and names Block, Inc. as the bitcoin provider. Block publishes its NMLS ID, 942933, alongside a state-by-state table ([Cash App on X](https://x.com/CashApp/status/1953845077861969932?lang=en); [Block Licenses](https://block.xyz/legal/licenses)). Equally instructive is what these companies avoid saying. **None claims that crypto is insured.** Coinbase describes USD pass-through coverage with conditions ("up to the per-depositor limit," dependent on accurate records), while third-party sites flatten that into an overclaim ([Coinbase User Agreement](https://www.coinbase.com/legal/user_agreement/united_states); [wealthvieu.com](https://wealthvieu.com/banking/coinbase/is-safe/)). The exact footer text, its placement, and whether NMLS IDs appear in sitewide footers were not verified for any of the three.

The legal guardrails explain why this caution matters. FDIC Part 328, amended in December 2023, bars "any person" from misrepresenting deposit insurance. It adds crypto-assets to the "non-deposit product" definition and treats it as a material omission when a nonbank fails to identify its insured bank conspicuously ([FDIC notice](https://www.fdic.gov/system/files/2024-07/2023-12-20-notice-dis-b-fr.pdf)). The FDIC sent cease-and-desist letters over insurance claims to FTX US and others starting in August 2022 ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/fdic-calls-out-ftx-us-other-crypto-firms-over-insurance-claims)). The FTC's case against Celsius targeted "safer than banks" and "$750M insurance" claims, and in **July 2026 the founders were ordered to pay $16.5M** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/07/founders-celsius-network-ordered-pay-165-million-resolve-ftc-charges)). The FTC's August 2024 rule bans fake or AI-generated reviews and testimonials, with civil penalties of roughly **$51,744 per violation** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Whether that rule has been amended since 2024 was not checked. For a site running on mock data, the conclusion is direct: **no testimonials, user counts, volume statistics, partner logos or "trusted by" strips until real ones exist.** Leave honest empty space in their place.

The required risk language has a clear template. New York's BitLicense rule, 23 NYCRR § 200.19(a), requires clear, conspicuous disclosure before the first transaction that virtual currency "is not legal tender, is not backed by the government," and is not FDIC/SIPC protected ([Justia](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-23/chapter-i/part-200/section-200-19)). The 2014 proposal also listed receipt contents: complaint line, transaction type, value, date and time, fee, exchange rate, liability for non-delivery and refund policy. The final receipt text was not verified ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/2455602/software/new-yorks-proposed-bitcoin-rules-include-consumer-protections.html)). Scam warnings have a firm evidence base. FTC data show Bitcoin ATM scam losses rose nearly tenfold from 2020 to 2023, people over 60 were **more than 3x as likely** to report a loss, and the median loss in the first half of 2024 was $10,000 ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/new-ftc-data-shows-massive-increase-losses-bitcoin-atm-scams)). That data covers cash-in scams. No cash-out-specific notice was found. Even so, a scam interstitial before the first sale is reasonable practice, saying that no agency, bank or tech-support company will ask you to convert crypto to cash for them. The links it uses (reportfraud.ftc.gov, ic3.gov) still need to be verified.

Applied to a pre-launch mock site, the leaders' trust patterns turn into honesty patterns. The site needs a persistent banner: "Demonstration prototype. Not a real service. No transactions are processed." The licenses page keeps the leaders' structure (legal entity, NMLS ID, state table, complaint contacts) but fills it with **clearly marked empty values**, such as "NMLS ID: — (none; not licensed)." It must never show a made-up number. Copy should be conditional ("a licensed operator would require…", "planned, subject to licensing"), and every figure should be labeled illustrative. Some words should not appear anywhere on the site, because each implies licensing, insurance, guarantees or anonymity that a mock cannot back up: "licensed," "regulated," "FDIC-insured," "insured," "bank-grade," "safe," "guaranteed rate," "instant settlement," "no KYC," "anonymous," "discreet," "no limits." The same goes for regulator logos and state seals. The leaders also show that state eligibility belongs inside the product, not only on legal pages. Kraken's ACH withdrawals are unavailable to New York residents, and several Cash App bitcoin features are excluded in New York ([Kraken ACH](https://support.kraken.com/articles/4408922630676-ach-withdrawals); [Cash App press](https://cash.app/press/cash-unlocks-bitcoin-everyday-stablecoins)). A state selector early in onboarding, even a mock one that returns "not available in any state," teaches users the right mental model.

## Courier cash and large tickets are compliance features, not conveniences

The courier-cash option changes the product's risk profile more than anything else on the site. A business that exchanges convertible virtual currency for USD is a money transmitter under the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA). It must register with FinCEN as a money services business (MSB) within 180 days of starting operations. That registration is **not a license**, and unlicensed transmission is a federal crime under 18 U.S.C. 1960 ([FinCEN CVC Guidance](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20CVC%20Guidance%20FINAL.pdf); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/blockchain-legal-resource/fincen-announces-first-enforcement-action-against-peer-to-peer-virtual-currency-exchange); [Ridgeway](https://www.ridgewayfs.com/money-transmitter-license-guide/)). Cash paid out over **$10,000 per person per business day must be aggregated and reported** on a currency transaction report (CTR), across all agents, and splitting amounts to avoid that is structuring, which is illegal in its own right ([eCFR Part 1022](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1022/subpart-C); [FinCEN Ruling 2005-6](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/administrative_ruling/fincenruling2005-6.pdf); [FinCEN Western Union](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/enforcement_action/western_union_assessment.pdf)). FinCEN's Southwest Border Geographic Targeting Orders lowered MSB cash-reporting thresholds to **$1,000** in targeted ZIP codes, and the September 2, 2026 reissue runs to March 1, 2027 ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/260911-fincen-reissues-geographic-targeting-order)). The closest US precedent is enforcement, not a product: in 2019 FinCEN penalized Eric Powers, an in-person cash bitcoin trader who filed no CTRs on 200+ cash transactions over $10,000 ([Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/regulatory-investigations-financial-crime-insights/2019/04/us-department-of-treasurys-fincen-penalizes-individual-operating-peer-to-peer.html)). **No US precedent of a licensed exchange offering doorstep cash delivery was found.** It also remains unresolved whether a third-party courier counts as an agent of the MSB.

Physical delivery adds further regulatory regimes. New York requires armored-car carriers to hold a state license and at least **$10M of all-risk insurance** ([NY DOS](https://dos.ny.gov/get-armored-car-carrier-license)). CertiK, a vendor source, counted a record **72 crypto "wrench attacks" worldwide in 2025** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/356604/41m-in-losses-as-crypto-wrench-attacks-hit-record-high-in-2025)). The UX should therefore treat courier cash as a gated concept, not a headline feature. It should be labeled "Concept only — not offered." Its gating should state that full identity verification is required before any cash quote, that ID is re-verified at handoff, that limits apply and are lower than the limits for bank payouts, and that cash payouts above reporting thresholds are reported to the government. The page must never suggest that cash is available "in an hour." It must also avoid the words that read as red flags for illicit finance: "no ID," "anonymous," "discreet," "no reporting." The 2025–2026 state crypto-kiosk statutes (California's $1,000 daily cap upheld in court; Illinois, Maine, Connecticut, Alabama and others with new-customer caps, fee caps and refund windows) offer reasonable default safeguards, such as lower limits for new customers and cooling-off holds ([DFPI ruling](https://dfpi.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/337/2024/09/24-09-04-Crypto-Kiosk-Ruling.pdf); [PYMNTS/CPI](https://www.pymnts.com/cpi-posts/states-move-to-rein-in-crypto-kiosks-as-fraud-concerns-mount/)). The analogy is imperfect, because kiosk laws target cash-in scams, not cash-out.

The deposit flow needs one disclosure that the leaders' consumer flows do not visibly emphasize: **incoming crypto is screened.** OFAC's October 2021 guidance expects screening of customers, IP and geolocation data, and wallet addresses against the SDN list, which has included crypto addresses since 2018. Liability is strict, and blockchain analytics are expected, though no specific tool is mandated ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ofac-publishes-sanctions-compliance-guidance-virtual-currency-industry); [Guidehouse](https://guidehouse.com/insights/financial-crimes/2021/ofac-sanctions-compliance-virtual-currency)). The site should never promise that every deposit will be paid out. It should state that flagged funds may be held, rejected or reported, and that payouts may be delayed for compliance review. The large-ticket path follows the same logic. OTC desks risk-rate clients, require source-of-funds and source-of-wealth documents, pre-register and screen settlement wallets, and cap trade size until a history exists ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/corpus/gen-2630/over-the-counter-finance/otc-crypto-desk-due-diligence-and-transaction-monitoring-controls.html)). Coinbase publishes source-of-wealth document requirements for high-net-worth individuals ([Coinbase HNWI onboarding](https://help.coinbase.com/en/business-onboarding/documents/hnwi)). The original site's large-transaction page should be a request-for-quote form that starts from the trade, lists the review steps ahead, gives no guaranteed timeline, and **defaults to bank wire**. Those sources are vendor content. No US regulator guidance specific to OTC due diligence was found.

The regulatory landscape is also moving, and the notes could not pin it down. California's DFAL licensing became operative July 1, 2026, and SB 97 amended it on June 30. A new entrant probably cannot serve California until licensed, but that is unconfirmed ([DFPI](https://dfpi.ca.gov/financial-services/digital-financial-assets-law/); [Orrick](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2026-07-17/california-enacts-amendments-to-digital-financial-assets-law-as-licensing-requirements-take-effect-july-1/)). Counts of Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA) adoption conflict: about 35 states per a summary of CSBS figures, versus Vixio's narrower count ([Business Law Today](https://businesslawtoday.org/?p=36626); [Vixio](https://www.vixio.com/articles/money-transmission-modernization-act-adoption-across-us-states)). FinCEN's AML program rule is still a proposal (April 2026 NPRM), with no final rule found ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/10/2026-07033/anti-money-laundering-and-countering-the-financing-of-terrorism-programs)).

### What remains unverified

Several items the notes flagged are still open and should not be designed around until checked. On the design side: Coinbase's 2026 hero, CTAs and nav; any leader's quote countdown, receipt layout or status screens; the leaders' actual figure sizes, number formatting and imagery; official brand hexes and type specs for all reference brands; Cash App's sub-$2,000 sell-fee tiers and whether its instant-transfer fee has a cap; Coinbase's instant-cashout fee; and whether Behance or Dribbble hold credible light-theme exchange concepts. On the regulatory side: the MSB SAR threshold, the CTR filing deadline and the MSB re-registration cycle (all recalled from memory, none confirmed against a source); whether Regulation E or the Remittance Transfer Rule applies to the USD payout leg; the final NY receipt text; the DFAL disclosure provisions for licensees that are not kiosks; whether the 2020 unhosted-wallet proposal was ever finalized; the status of the FDIC digital-signage provisions (compliance dates pushed to 2027); the CFPB's current posture; and whether the CLARITY or GENIUS Acts change federal exchange licensing. A direct visual audit of the live reference sites, and counsel review of every disclosure string, are prerequisites for moving past mock data.

## Conclusion

The research points to an unexpected source of differentiation. The leaders' weak spots in cash-out UX are mostly about **disclosure and structure**, not visual polish: spread in tooltips, two transactions where users want one, and fee figures that drift between pages. Those weaknesses are cheap for a new entrant to fix and hard for the incumbents to undo. The same discipline that makes the quote card honest (one canonical fee source, hedged times, no unearned claims) is what US regulators already enforce through FDIC, FTC and state actions. For this product, then, good UX and compliance readiness are the same work.

For a site running on mock data, the useful reframe is that **visible gaps build credibility**. A licenses page with labeled empty fields, a courier option marked "not offered," and a testimonials section that does not exist yet all mark the product as serious, and they keep the prototype from becoming evidence of misrepresentation later. The design system should be built so that real values, such as an NMLS ID, a partner bank name or live rates, drop into slots that already exist, rather than replacing placeholders written to look real.
